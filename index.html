Project Overview

Abstract

Work in Progress

We present an augmented reality brain-robot interface (AR-BRI) for generalist robot arm manipulation, combining gaze-based object selection with EEG-based motor imagery control. The system enables intuitive interaction within a shared autonomy framework, allowing users to perform multi-step daily tasks directly in their physical environment.

Through a user study involving activities of daily living such as drinking, using a drawer, and operating an oven, we demonstrate effective task execution, high user engagement, and good usability, highlighting the potential of AR-based interfaces for assistive robotics.

Motivation

Work in Progress

Assistive robotics aims to support individuals with physical impairments in performing activities of daily living (ADLs), such as drinking, cooking, or manipulating everyday objects. However, existing systems are often limited to predefined tasks and rely on interfaces that are not intuitive, requiring users to divide their attention between control devices and the physical environment.

In this work, we explore a more natural interaction paradigm by combining augmented reality with brain-robot interfaces. By integrating gaze-based object selection and EEG-based motor imagery control directly within the user’s environment, our system enables intuitive, hands-free interaction with a robotic arm for flexible, multi-step tasks.

System Design

Work in progress. The proposed AR brain-robot interface combines gaze-based object selection, zero-shot object detection, EEG-based motor imagery decoding, and shared autonomy robot control. Users interact directly with objects in the physical workspace through spatial AR overlays, while the robot executes the selected high-level actions using autonomous manipulation policies.

User Study

Work in progress. We conducted a user study to evaluate the effectiveness, usability, and interaction capabilities of our augmented reality brain–robot interface for generalist manipulation tasks. The study aimed to assess whether users could reliably control a robotic arm through a combination of gaze and EEG-based motor imagery, and to what extent the system supports sequential task execution in realistic scenarios.

A total of 18 participants took part in the experiment, with varying levels of prior experience with brain-computer interfaces. Each session included a calibration phase for training the EEG decoder, followed by a short practice period to familiarise participants with the interaction paradigm.

Participants were then asked to complete a set of multi-step manipulation tasks representative of activities of daily living. We evaluated system performance across multiple dimensions, including task success rate, completion time, and EEG decoding accuracy. In addition, subjective measures were collected using standardised questionnaires, including the System Usability Scale (SUS) and the NASA Task Load Index (NASA-TLX), to assess perceived usability and workload.

These metrics allowed us to capture both objective system performance and user experience, providing a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed interaction framework.

Results

Work in progress. We evaluated the proposed AR brain-robot interface through a user study assessing system performance, usability, and cognitive workload. Overall, participants successfully completed multi-step manipulation tasks with high reliability and reported positive interaction experiences.

System Usability and Workload Evaluation

🔍

NASA-TLX

Workload Analysis

Participants reported a cognitively engaging but physically undemanding interaction. Mental demand and effort were the dominant workload contributors, while physical demand, temporal demand, and frustration remained relatively low.

🔍

SUS

Usability Evaluation

The system achieved an overall SUS score of 76.94, corresponding to good usability. Participants rated the interface as easy to learn, straightforward, and well integrated for AR-based brain-robot interaction.

Takeaways

Work in progress.
Key insights from the proposed AR brain-robot interface:

  • Intuitive multimodal interaction — Gaze, EEG, and AR enable natural, hands-free robot control with minimal physical input.
  • Intent-to-action mapping — High-level human intentions are directly translated into low-level robotic execution for efficient task completion.
  • Generalist manipulation — A single robot policy supports diverse everyday tasks without task-specific programming.
  • Balanced cognitive demand — EEG introduces effort, but gaze and autonomy reduce overall mental workload.
  • Positive usability — Users reported good experience and high system acceptability in interaction tasks.
  • Assistive autonomy potential — The system supports future ADLs assistance for users with motor impairments.
